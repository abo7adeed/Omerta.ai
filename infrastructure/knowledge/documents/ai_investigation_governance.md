# Section 1: Permitted Uses and Boundaries of Autonomous AI

1.1. Scope of AI Deployment
Autonomous AI agents within Omerta.ai are authorized strictly for:
- Forensic inquiry understanding and query decomposition.
- Read-only data retrieval and evidence correlation across PostgreSQL and Neo4j.
- Statistical anomaly detection, period comparisons, and trend analysis.
- Drafting preliminary Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR) and executive summaries.

1.2. Strict Non-Autonomous Action Prohibition
AI models and agents are strictly forbidden from:
- Directly executing funds transfers or ledger modifications.
- Unilaterally freezing, unblocking, or altering account statuses.
- Modifying customer authentication credentials or transfer passwords.
- Executing arbitrary shell scripts, unrestricted SQL queries, or arbitrary Python commands.

# Section 2: Evidence Provenance and Citation Requirements

2.1. Mandatory Provenance Tracking
Every claim, metric, or finding generated by an agent must be backed by an unambiguous citation citing:
- For Document RAG: Document ID, version, section heading, and chunk hash.
- For PostgreSQL: Exact entity ID, transaction external ID, or aggregated query time window.
- For Neo4j: Explicit nodes, shared hardware identifiers, or graph traversal path descriptors.

2.2. Zero-Hallucination Invariant
If requested data is absent, ambiguous, or insufficient, the AI agent must explicitly output an INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE or NEEDS_CLARIFICATION status rather than fabricating metrics, counterparties, or statutory provisions.

# Section 3: Limitations of Telemetry and Graph Signals

3.1. Probabilistic vs. Deterministic Signals
- IP geolocation, VPN telemetry, and device fingerprint matches represent probabilistic structural observations, not conclusive legal evidence of guilt.
- Shared devices may arise innocently from household family members or shared small-business hardware.
- The AI agent must always present structural graph observations as hypotheses requiring human contextual validation.
